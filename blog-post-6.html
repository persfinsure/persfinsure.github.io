Parents Visiting USA this December? Health Cover You Need

Published on November 23, 2026

Your parents land in New Jersey, Texas or California in a few weeks. They will see the Christmas lights, the first snowfall and their grandchildren opening presents. What nobody plans for is a slip on an icy driveway, a bad chest infection or a sudden spike in blood pressure in the middle of the holidays.

In the USA, that one moment can turn into a hospital bill larger than the whole trip. This guide explains, in plain words, how visitor insurance for parents visiting the USA works, what a US-issued visitor plan covers, and what to check before your parents board the flight this December.

Quick answer

If your parents are visiting the USA this winter, buy a US visitor medical plan before they leave home, with cover starting on the day they arrive. Check four things: the sum insured (the most the plan will pay), the deductible (what you pay first), how the plan treats pre-existing conditions, and whether it has a PPO network for direct billing. A good visitor plan does not make US health care free, but it can stop one emergency from becoming a financial crisis. Cover is always subject to the insurer's policy terms, conditions and exclusions.

  • NRI and Indian-origin children in the USA hosting parents for the December holidays
  • Parents travelling on a B1/B2 visitor visa for a few weeks or several months
  • Visitors from any country, not only India, who will stay with family in the USA
  • Families whose parents are already booked and who have not yet sorted insurance

Why December is a higher-risk month for visiting parents

Winter in much of the USA is very different from winter in India. Parents who have never seen snow are often surprised by how hard the cold hits.

  • Falls on ice and snow. Driveways, steps and parking lots can freeze overnight. A fall can mean a fracture, an X-ray, a specialist and sometimes surgery.
  • Flu, RSV and chest infections. December and January are peak season for respiratory illness in the USA. Older visitors, and those with diabetes, asthma or heart conditions, can be hit harder.
  • Heart and blood pressure strain. Cold weather, long flights, jet lag and rich holiday food can all put pressure on the heart.
  • Holiday travel inside the USA. Many families drive or fly to other cities for Christmas and New Year. More travel means more chances of an accident or illness away from home.
  • Holiday closures. Your regular doctor's office may be closed. That often means a visit to urgent care or the emergency room (ER), which cost more.

None of this is a reason to cancel the trip. It is a reason to make sure the right cover is in place before it starts.

How US health care bills visitors

In India, many families are used to paying a hospital bill in cash or claiming later from a domestic policy. The US system works differently, and it helps to understand it before you need it.

There is no public health system for visitors. Visitors on a B1/B2 visa are not eligible for Medicare or Medicaid, the US government health programmes. They pay for care themselves, or through a private visitor plan.

Hospitals bill the patient or the insurer. After treatment, the hospital, the doctors, the lab and the ambulance may each send a separate bill. It is common to receive several bills for one visit.

Prices are high, and they vary a lot. An ER visit with tests and scans can run into thousands of dollars. A hospital stay or surgery can run into tens of thousands. Prices differ by city, hospital and treatment, so it is hard to predict a bill in advance.

Emergency rooms must treat you first. US hospitals with emergency departments must assess and stabilise anyone with an emergency, whether or not they can pay. The bill still arrives afterwards.

Without insurance, the family is responsible. Hospitals may ask for payment, set up a payment plan or send unpaid bills to collection agencies. That is a stressful way to end a family holiday.

What US-based visitor insurance is

A US visitor medical plan is a short-term health insurance policy for people who are visiting the USA and are not US residents. PersFinSure helps families compare visitor plans that are issued in the USA, for people visiting the USA from anywhere in the world.

These plans are built for the way US health care works. In general, a visitor plan may include:

  • Doctor visits, urgent care and ER visits for a new illness or injury during the trip
  • Hospital stays, surgery, tests, X-rays and scans for covered conditions
  • Ambulance charges, within the plan's limits
  • Prescription drugs for a covered illness or injury, depending on the plan
  • Emergency medical evacuation to the nearest suitable hospital, where included
  • Repatriation, meaning help to return the patient home for further treatment, or of remains, where included
  • Limited cover for an acute onset of a pre-existing condition on some plans (more on this below)
  • A 24/7 assistance helpline to help find a doctor or hospital

Benefits and limits differ between plans and insurers. Always read the plan's policy wording or certificate before you buy.

Fixed plans vs comprehensive plans

US visitor plans usually come in two broad types. Knowing the difference is one of the most useful things you can learn before comparing.

How they payA set dollar amount for each service, e.g. a fixed amount per hospital day or per doctor visitA share of the actual eligible bill, after the deductible and any co-insurance, up to the sum insured
PremiumUsually lowerUsually higher
Risk to the familyYou may pay a large part of a big bill if it is higher than the fixed amountsLower, because the plan pays a share of the real bill
NetworkOften a PPO network for access, but payments are still limited to the scheduleUsually a PPO network with direct billing at network providers
Best suited toBudget-conscious families, shorter trips, younger and healthy visitorsOlder parents, longer stays, and families who want more predictable protection

There is no single right answer. A fixed plan can look cheaper, but the gap between the fixed amount and the actual US bill is paid by the family. A comprehensive plan costs more, but usually leaves less to pay after a serious claim. We will look at this comparison in more depth in a later post.

How much cover do your parents need?

The sum insured, also called the policy maximum, is the most the plan will pay for covered medical costs during the policy period. Visitor plans usually offer a range of limits, and you choose one when you buy.

Things that should push you towards a higher limit:

  • Age. Parents aged 65 and above are more likely to need hospital care.
  • Trip length. A four-month stay carries more risk than a two-week holiday.
  • Health history. Long-term conditions raise the chance of a claim, even if they are well controlled.
  • Where they will stay. Care in large metro areas is often priced higher.
  • Travel within the USA. More cities and more driving means more exposure.

Many families find that a very low limit gives a false sense of safety. One hospital stay with surgery can use up a small limit quickly. The right figure depends on your parents and your budget. Terms to understand before you compare

US insurance uses some words that may be new to Indian families. Here they are in plain language.

  • Deductible: the amount you pay yourself before the plan starts paying. A higher deductible usually means a lower premium. Some plans apply it once per policy; others apply it per illness or injury.
  • Co-insurance: after the deductible, the share of the bill you pay. For example, with 80/20 co-insurance the plan pays 80% and you pay 20% of eligible costs, often up to a set limit.
  • Co-pay: a fixed amount you pay for some services, such as an urgent care or ER visit.
  • Out-of-pocket maximum: on some comprehensive plans, the most you pay in deductible and co-insurance before the plan pays 100% of eligible costs up to the limit.
  • PPO network: a group of doctors and hospitals that have agreed rates with the plan. Using a network provider can mean direct billing and lower bills.
  • Direct billing: the provider sends the bill to the insurer instead of asking you to pay first. This is the US version of what Indian families call "cashless". It is not guaranteed in every case, even at network providers.
  • Usual, customary and reasonable (UCR): the amount the insurer considers a normal charge for a service in that area. Charges above it may not be paid in full.
  • Waiting period: a period after the policy starts during which some benefits, often illness, are not covered. It usually applies when a plan is bought after arrival.

Reading the plan with these terms in mind will tell you far more than comparing premiums alone.

Pre-existing conditions: read this part carefully

This is the section families ask us about most. Many parents have conditions such as diabetes, high blood pressure, thyroid problems or heart disease.

Most US visitor plans do not cover pre-existing conditions in full. Routine care for them, such as check-ups, regular medicines and refills, is usually excluded.

Some plans offer cover for an acute onset of a pre-existing condition. This generally means a sudden and unexpected flare-up of a condition that was stable, which needs urgent treatment within a short time. Such cover usually comes with:

  • A sub-limit, meaning a lower maximum than the main sum insured
  • Age limits, as some plans offer it only up to a certain age
  • Conditions, such as the treatment being received within a set number of hours
  • Exclusions, such as known worsening before the trip or planned treatment

Definitions differ between plans, so read the wording. No plan should be assumed to cover every existing condition in full, and whether a claim is paid depends on the medical facts. Our guide to understanding pre-existing condition coverage for Indian parents visiting the USA goes deeper.

Practical tips for parents with long-term conditions

  1. Get a check-up in India a few weeks before the flight, and keep the reports.
  2. Carry enough regular medicine for the whole stay, with prescriptions in English.
  3. Carry a short summary of conditions, medicines and allergies on paper and on the phone.
  4. Answer every medical question on the application honestly. Wrong or missing details can lead to a claim being reduced or refused.

Already booked for December? Act now

If your parents fly in a few weeks, here is a simple timeline.

Three to four weeks before the flight

  • Confirm exact travel dates and passport names.
  • Ask your parents' Indian health insurer, in writing, whether the policy covers anything in the USA.
  • List medical conditions and medicines.

Two weeks before the flight

  • Compare US visitor plans: sum insured, deductible, co-insurance, network and pre-existing condition terms.
  • Choose a start date that matches the arrival date in the USA.
  • Buy the plan, and check that names, dates of birth and passport details are correct.

The week of the flight

  • Save the policy number, ID card and 24/7 helpline on your parents' phones and yours.
  • Note two or three network urgent care centres and hospitals near your home.
  • Keep a printed copy of the ID card in your parents' hand luggage.

If your parents have already arrived: some visitor plans can still be bought after arrival, but a waiting period for illness may apply, and anything that started before the policy began will not be covered. Buying before travel is always safer.

Does your parents' Indian policy help in the USA?

Families often ask whether an Indian health or travel policy is enough. The honest answer is "it depends, so check".

  • Indian domestic health policies usually cover treatment in India only. Some newer plans offer limited global cover, often only for emergencies or planned treatment with conditions.
  • Indian overseas travel policies bought in India can cover medical emergencies abroad. Check the limit, how claims are paid in the USA and how pre-existing conditions are treated.
  • Global health plans cover treatment in several countries, including India. These can suit parents who visit the USA every year, and they are a different product from a short visitor plan.

When something happens: how claims work

Knowing the steps in advance makes a stressful moment easier.

  1. In a real emergency, call 911 first. Insurance can be sorted after your parent is safe.
  2. For non-emergencies, call the plan's helpline. They can point you to a network urgent care centre or doctor, which is usually far cheaper than an ER.
  3. Show the insurance ID card at registration and ask whether the provider will bill the insurer directly.
  4. Inform the insurer quickly if your parent is admitted to hospital. Some plans require notice within a set time.
  5. Keep every paper. Bills, receipts, discharge summaries, prescriptions and the doctor's notes.
  6. Submit a claim form if you paid anything yourself. Most plans let you do this online or by email.
  7. Read the Explanation of Benefits (EOB). This statement shows what the plan paid and what is left for you. Check it against the hospital bill before paying anything more.

Do not ignore a US medical bill, even if you expect insurance to pay. If something looks wrong, call the provider and the insurer, or message us and we will help you understand it.

A worked example (illustrative only)

The figures below are hypothetical and are not a quote, a premium or a promise of cover. Actual bills, benefits and claim decisions vary.

Rohan, 35, lives near Chicago. His parents, aged 67 and 62, arrive from Pune on 5 December for a three-month stay. His father has controlled high blood pressure.

Rohan buys a comprehensive US visitor plan two weeks before the flight. He chooses a $100,000 sum insured, a deductible and 80/20 co-insurance up to a set out-of-pocket limit. The plan includes limited acute onset cover for pre-existing conditions.

On 28 December, after a snowfall, his father slips on the front steps. An ambulance takes him to a network hospital. He has a fractured wrist, which needs surgery and a one-night stay.

Suppose the combined bills for the ambulance, ER, imaging, surgery and stay come to $32,000.

  • Without visitor insurance: the family is responsible for the full $32,000, and the bills may arrive from several providers.
  • With a fixed benefit plan: the plan pays its set amounts for each service. If those add up to much less than the real bill, the family pays the difference, which could still be large.
  • With the comprehensive plan in this example: after the deductible, the plan pays 80% of eligible costs until Rohan reaches the out-of-pocket limit, then pays eligible costs in full up to the $100,000 maximum. Rohan's family pays the deductible, the co-insurance up to that limit and anything the policy excludes.

Now suppose that in January his father has a sudden spike in blood pressure and needs emergency care. Whether this is covered depends on the plan's acute onset definition, the sub-limit and the medical history he declared. This is why reading the pre-existing condition wording matters as much as the price.

Eight checks before you buy

  1. Dates and names match the passport exactly.
  2. Sum insured is high enough for your parents' age, health and length of stay.
  3. Deductible, co-insurance and out-of-pocket limit are clear, not just the premium.
  4. Fixed or comprehensive? You know which type you are buying and what it means for a large bill.
  5. Pre-existing and acute onset wording has been read, including age limits and sub-limits.
  6. PPO network includes hospitals and urgent care near where your parents will stay.
  7. Evacuation and repatriation are included, or you have decided you do not need them.
  8. Extension and cancellation rules are clear, in case the trip runs longer or the visa is refused.

Frequently asked questions

Is visitor insurance mandatory for parents on a B1/B2 visa?
It is not a general visa requirement, but it is strongly advised. Without it, your family pays for any medical care in the USA directly.

Can we buy visitor insurance after my parents arrive in the USA?
Often yes, but many plans then apply a waiting period for illness, and conditions that began earlier are excluded. Buying before travel is safer.

Does visitor insurance cover diabetes or blood pressure?
Usually not routine care or regular medicines. Some plans cover a sudden acute flare-up of a stable condition, with a sub-limit and age limits. Read the policy wording.

What is the difference between fixed and comprehensive plans?
A fixed plan pays set amounts for each service, so a large bill can leave a big gap. A comprehensive plan pays a share of the actual eligible bill after the deductible, up to the sum insured.

Is treatment cashless in the USA?
At many network providers, the bill goes directly to the insurer. This is called direct billing. It is not guaranteed everywhere, and you may still need to pay the deductible or co-pay.

Can we extend the plan if my parents stay longer?
Many plans allow an extension if you ask before the policy ends. Contact us or the insurer early.
